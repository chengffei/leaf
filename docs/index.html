What is Leaf?

Leaf is a free, open-source ebook reader for Mac. Double-click an EPUB, MOBI or AZW3 file to read it; close the window and Leaf quits. No library, no copies, no account. It remembers where you stopped, and reads like a Kindle. Native SwiftUI app, about 2.7 MB, macOS 15 or later. Not affiliated with Amazon.

Install

Leaf isn’t notarized by Apple. Allow it once on first launch.

Download Leaf.dmg

Drag to Applications

Open Leaf.dmg and drag Leaf into the Applications folder.

Open it once

Double-click Leaf. When macOS says it can’t verify the developer, click Done.

Allow in Settings

In System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway.

Requires macOS 15 or later. Books with DRM, such as Kindle Store purchases, can’t be opened.

FAQ

Common questions about installing and using Leaf.

How do I open MOBI and AZW3 files on a Mac?

Install Leaf, then double-click the file in Finder, or right-click it and choose Open With › Leaf. The file must be DRM-free.

Is there a Kindle-like ebook reader for Mac?

Leaf reads DRM-free EPUB, MOBI and AZW3 files the Kindle way: time left from your own reading speed, location numbers, typography presets, footnotes in place and find in book. Highlights and notes come next. No store, no account.

How is Leaf different from Readest?

Readest is cross-platform, with a library, sync, highlights and text-to-speech. Leaf is Mac only and does less on purpose: no library, only your reading position, and it quits when you close the book. About 2.7 MB, against 67 MB.

Is Leaf free?

Yes. Leaf is free and open source under the MIT License.

Why does macOS say it can’t verify the developer?

Leaf isn’t notarized by Apple. Open it once and dismiss the warning, then go to System Settings › Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. You only need to do this once.

Can Leaf open Kindle books I bought?

Only DRM-free files. Books bought from the Kindle Store usually have DRM, and Leaf can’t open them.

Is Leaf a Kindle app?

No. Leaf is an independent open-source project, not affiliated with Amazon. It borrows the reading experience of a Kindle — typography, progress, find, and soon highlights and notes — for DRM-free EPUB, MOBI and AZW3 files on your Mac. There is no store and no account.

Does Leaf have a library or bookshelf?

No. Leaf opens books where they are, without importing or copying them. Leaf itself keeps only where you stopped in each book; like any Mac document app, macOS lists recent files under File › Open Recent.
